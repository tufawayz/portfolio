I build AI agents and automations at Pfizer Italy, in the Digital & Technology team. I take a use case from the first conversation with the people who do the job to a prototype tested on real cases.

I have used generative AI for over four years. Before this I scouted AI startups and coordinated Proofs of Concept for large companies, and before that I worked in marketing and sales.

How I build an agent

  1. Requirements. I sit with the people who do the job and map the process as it is.
  2. Scope. I pick the smallest part worth automating and define the MVP.
  3. Instructions and sources. I write what the agent must do and which documents it may use. Controlled sources only.
  4. Real cases. I run it on real cases and compare the output with the source, line by line.
  5. A person decides. The agent flags and suggests. It does not approve, reject or send.

What one looks like

agent: expense-doc-checker
platform: Microsoft 365 Copilot
input: expense documentation
knowledge: rules and reference cases      # controlled sources only
steps:
  - read the document
  - compare with the rules
  - flag anomalies and missing information
guardrail: the authorised operator decides
evaluation: real cases, reading errors fixed one by one

Training and e-learning

Building an agent is half of the work. The other half is getting people to use it.

  • Live Copilot training for the field force. Co-designed and delivered with two colleagues. I ran the live demos and the prompting exercises.
  • Two Storyline modules. The first turns that live session into a self-paced module. Built with triggers, states, drag-and-drop and a graded quiz.
  • Localisation and QA. Italian version of an AI fundamentals course: subtitle and text review, with corrections delivered in a format the course team could apply directly.
